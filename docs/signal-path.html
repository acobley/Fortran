Five things that catch people out

All of these are the DXi's, and all of them are measured rather than assumed.

  • The filter isn't a filterstages A–C

    There's no time-domain filter in the additive path at all. The 552 bins sit on a quarter-semitone grid, bin 276 pinned to 440 Hz, spanning 8.18 Hz to 23.3 kHz; each partial reads the table at its own offset. The envelope moves the bin, downward, from 440 Hz.

  • The ladder barely filtersstage 6

    Measured: a flat +10.6 dB, a −3 dB corner at 0.198 × Nyquist, and 0.22 dB of resonance — so not really a ladder, just a four-pole low pass. And the synth has only ten partials, so it only reaches that corner above about B4. The worst any partial moves when you switch it out:

    C2, 65 Hz0.06 dBnothing at all
    A3, 220 Hz0.24 dBnothing at all
    A4, 440 Hz1.97 dBjust audible
    C6, 1047 Hz21.44 dBa real filter

    Its corner also moves with the sample rate — the coefficients are never scaled — so the same patch is over an octave brighter at 96 k than at 44.1 k. That one is a defect, and it is not fixed.

  • Pan was never wrongstage 7

    Worth recording because this page said the opposite. The DXi's L = √(2(1−n/128)), R = √(2n/128) is a textbook constant-power law — L²+R² = 2.000 at every position, so a note is equally loud wherever it sits. Only the normalisation was unusual: unity at the centre, leaving a hard-panned note peaking 3.01 dB above a centred one. Divided by √2 now, so unity sits at the ends and panning can never add peak level. The taper is untouched.

  • The wheel bends an octavestage 1

    2^((wheel − ½) × 2), so the end stops are ×½ and ×2 — six times the usual ±2 semitones, and deliberate.

  • The headroom cancels itselfstages 5 & 6

    The voice gain carries × 0.25 under the original's own comment “allow for 12dB of headroom” — and the ladder ends × 5 four lines later. Net ×1.25, above unity. One note at the factory patch peaked at +9.4 dBFS; sixteen reached +25.7 dB. It also flattened velocity completely: everything from 16 upward landed above the host's ceiling and came out at the same level. Hence the trim, which defaults 20 dB down.
