Inside the partial bank

One sine, two branches: what decides where it is in its cycle, and what decides how loud. Then ten of them, differing only in a number.

One partial

All ten are this, with k running 1 to 10.

What actually differs between the ten

Structurally they are identical. Four things carry the index.

  • The harmonic numberk × θ

    The only part that is not a control. Partial k runs at k times the note's frequency — so the tenth is at 4.4 kHz for a note at A4, which is what puts it in reach of the output ladder's corner.

  • Where it reads the tablebin + offsetₖ

    Each partial reads the filter's response at its own frequency, as an offset on the quarter-semitone grid. Those offsets are rounded to whole semitones — see below.

  • Its own five controlslevel, phase, invert

    Plus a whole ADSR with its own level, enable and invert, and an LFO with its own rate and depth. That is 12 parameters per partial, 120 of the plug-in's 145.

  • Nothing elsethe code is one loop

    There is no per-partial special case anywhere. The DXi wrote the loop out ten times by hand, which is why the port could fold it back into one.

The offsets are rounded, and it is audible

Harmonic n sits 12·log₂(n) semitones above the fundamental. The DXi rounds that to a whole semitone before multiplying by four for the quarter-semitone grid.

The seventh is read almost a third of a semitone sharp. It still sounds at exactly seven times the fundamental — the offset only chooses where in the filter's response its magnitude and phase come from. So a filter shape with any slope treats the seventh as if it were slightly higher than it is, and the fifth and tenth as slightly higher too. Everything else lands within four cents.

It is verbatim from the DXi's Values[], and the rounding is kept: it is part of the sound, and correcting it would change the timbre of every patch that engages a filter.
